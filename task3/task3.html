Alan Turing

1912-1954

About Alan Turing

Alan Turing (1912–1954) was an English mathematician, computer scientist, logician, and cryptanalyst who is widely considered to be the father of modern theoretical computer science and artificial intelligence.
During the Second World War, he played a pivotal role in cracking intercepted coded messages, most notably the German Enigma machine, which helped saved millions of lives.
He also invented the concept of the Turing machine, a mathematical model of computation that laid the theoretical foundation for all contemporary electronic computers.

Events

  1. 1936 — Publication of "On Computable Numbers"
  2. 1939 — Joining Bletchley Park
  3. 1945 — Joining the National Physical Laboratory (NPL)
  4. 1948 — Appointment at the University of Manchester
  5. 1951 — Election as a Fellow of the Royal Society

Achievements

  • Universal Turing Machine (1936)
  • The Bombe Machine (1939–1940)
  • Automatic Computing Engine / ACE (1945)
  • The Turing Test (1950)
  • Mathematical Theory of Morphogenesis (1952)
